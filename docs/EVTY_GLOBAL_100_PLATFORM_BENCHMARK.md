# EVTY Global Event Platform Benchmark — 100-Platform Landscape Scan

**Prepared:** 13 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Identify proven product patterns and defensible gaps for EVTY.  
**Scope note:** This is a curated landscape scan of 100 platforms and adjacent products, not a 100-vendor hands-on feature audit. Product positioning is summarised at category level; availability, pricing, market coverage and feature details must be revalidated against vendor documentation before procurement or competitive claims. Some entries are adjacent discovery, event-management, venue, or audience-engagement products rather than direct ticketing competitors.

## Executive direction

EVTY should aim to become a **trusted event-commerce and operations network**, not simply a ticket checkout page. Its differentiator should be the end-to-end connection of:

1. Discovery that works for local, niche and emerging events.
2. Organiser tools that manage the event lifecycle, not only ticket sales.
3. Safe, transparent ticket commerce with auditable money movement.
4. Reliable gate operations, including constrained-connectivity environments.
5. Promoter, sponsor and vendor workflows connected to attributable outcomes.
6. Post-event retention, community and repeat attendance.

The first launch market remains Nigeria. International expansion should be enabled by country, currency, timezone, tax, provider and localisation configuration—not by hard-coding Nigerian assumptions into every workflow.

## Defensible EVTY opportunities

These are hypotheses to validate with Nigerian organisers, attendees and vendors. They are not claims that no competitor offers them.

### 1. WhatsApp-native event commerce
- Shareable event cards and referral links that render cleanly in WhatsApp.
- Transactional updates through approved WhatsApp Business integrations, with email/SMS fallbacks.
- Group attendance: one buyer can coordinate multiple tickets, while each attendee receives an independently transferable credential.
- Low-bandwidth pages, compressed imagery and clear retry behaviour.

### 2. Trust and payment transparency
- Payment status timeline: initiated, pending, verified, failed, refunded.
- No ticket issuance from browser redirect; payment confirmation is server-side.
- Buyer-visible ticket ownership, transfer history and refund policy.
- Organiser verification levels and clear event cancellation/change notices.
- Reconciliation between provider transactions, EVTY orders and the internal ledger.

### 3. Reliable admission in weak-connectivity environments
- Device enrolment and event-scoped staff roles.
- Signed, short-lived scan authorisations and encrypted offline scan queues.
- Duplicate scans blocked locally where possible and reconciled centrally.
- Clear conflict handling when two devices scan the same ticket offline.
- Emergency revocation and a documented manual fallback.
- Do not promise fully secure offline admission until threat modelling and adversarial testing prove it.

### 4. Organiser command centre
- Event checklist, milestones, task ownership, run-of-show and incident log.
- Budget vs actual, vendor quotes, staffing and settlement statement.
- Ticket sales by tier, channel, promoter and time.
- Attendee exports with permission and privacy controls.
- Event change/cancellation communications and refund workflow.

### 5. Promoter and creator commerce
- Unique attributable referral links and QR codes.
- Commission rules fixed before campaign launch and snapshotted per eligible order.
- Fraud checks for self-referrals, duplicate devices, chargebacks and suspicious traffic.
- Promoter dashboard with clicks, paid conversions, pending/approved commission and payout status.
- Transparent advertiser/sponsored placement labelling.

### 6. Vendor marketplace with measurable outcomes
- Verified supplier profiles, service areas, portfolio and availability requests.
- Quote requests and event-specific vendor briefs.
- Organiser-side shortlist and comparison.
- Lead tracking and post-event vendor reviews.
- Keep vendor leads and payments separate from ticket money until a compliant vendor payment model is designed.

### 7. Better event discovery
- Search by location, date, price, category, accessibility, language and audience suitability.
- Curated collections and trusted local guides.
- Follow artists, venues, organisers and communities.
- Recurring events and series pages.
- Calendar save, map view, shareable plans and reminders.
- User controls for recommendations and notification frequency.

### 8. Accessibility and inclusion
- Screen-reader labels, keyboard navigation, contrast and scalable text.
- Event-level accessibility information: step-free access, seating, captions, quiet space and companion policy.
- Clear age restrictions, event language and content advisories.
- Accessible ticket selection and support contact.

### 9. Event intelligence
- Organiser dashboards that separate gross sales, fees, refunds, tax, promoter liabilities and payable balance.
- Campaign attribution with consent-aware analytics.
- Forecasts based on the organiser's own historical data, not fabricated benchmarks.
- Post-event report: attendance, no-show rate, scan throughput, conversion and customer feedback.

### 10. International-ready infrastructure
- Country/currency/timezone and locale configuration.
- Provider abstraction by market, with provider-specific verification and webhook contracts.
- Country-specific tax, refund and consumer disclosure rules reviewed before launch.
- Multi-language event content and local date/number formatting.

## EVTY product priorities

### P0 — Commercial and trust foundation (release blockers)
1. PostgreSQL migration, migrations in source control, backup and restore rehearsal.
2. Durable order state machine and expiring, atomic ticket inventory reservations.
3. Provider-independent payment interface; Paystack adapter remains a provider module, not a complete checkout.
4. Raw-body webhook intake, signature verification, durable idempotency and reconciliation.
5. Immutable double-entry ledger, accepted commission agreement snapshots, refunds and settlement controls.
6. Ticket issuance only after confirmed payment; secure credential hashing and atomic redemption.
7. Role-scoped gate scanner with duplicate-scan protection and audited offline policy.
8. End-to-end tests for race conditions, duplicate callbacks, partial refunds, provider timeouts and recovery.

### P1 — Launch experience
1. Attendee checkout, order history, ticket wallet and refund request UI.
2. Organiser event workspace: event setup, ticket tiers, orders, attendees, check-in and statements.
3. Admin trust/safety workspace: organiser verification, moderation, payment exceptions, disputes and audit.
4. Accessible, fast event discovery with maps, filters, follow/favourite and share flows.
5. Transactional notifications and event change/cancellation communication.

### P2 — Differentiation and marketplace growth
1. Promoter referrals and commission dashboard.
2. Vendor directory, quote requests and lead tracking.
3. Sponsored listings and campaign reporting with clear ad labels.
4. Waitlists and controlled ticket transfer/resale.
5. Community pages, recurring event series, group attendance and memberships.
6. Speaker, agenda, exhibitor and networking modules for conferences.
7. Event planning, run-of-show, supplier and staffing tools.

### P3 — Scale and international expansion
1. Android and iOS clients, including a gate-scanning app.
2. Multi-country payment/provider rollout and localisation.
3. Venue seating maps, season passes and subscriptions.
4. Advanced festival access, RFID integrations and cashless partner integrations.
5. API ecosystem, partner webhooks and approved integrations.

## Product principles and non-goals

- Do not copy competitor code, branding, screen layouts or proprietary text.
- Do not add blockchain or AI merely as marketing claims. Add them only where they solve a validated trust or workflow problem.
- Do not mix organiser funds, promoter commissions, tax liabilities and EVTY revenue.
- Do not allow payment provider callbacks to directly issue tickets without idempotent order and ledger checks.
- Do not introduce unreviewed offline ticket acceptance or uncontrolled resale.
- Do not enable live money movement until merchant, legal, accounting and operational requirements are confirmed.
- Do not claim “best in the world” as a fact. Compete through measured reliability, trust, conversion, organiser retention and attendee satisfaction.

## Validation plan

Before treating these opportunities as product requirements:
1. Interview at least 15 organisers across concerts, conferences, faith/community events, nightlife, sports and education.
2. Interview at least 20 attendees, including users with low-end Android devices and limited data.
3. Interview 8–10 promoters, vendors and gate operators.
4. Test clickable prototypes for discovery, checkout, transfer, check-in and organiser operations.
5. Measure task completion, abandonment, support burden, scan throughput and reconciliation accuracy.
6. Run a controlled pilot with free or sandbox transactions before enabling live ticket sales.
